The sender submits jobs one at a time; workers each hold a job for job duration then return to the receive. Time is stretched so you can see it — in your Go program the sends take microseconds and the jobs take a second.

Rules mirrored from Go: a send goes hand-to-hand if a worker is parked at the receive, otherwise into the buffer if there's room, otherwise the sender blocks. A blocked sender never cuts in line — when a worker receives, the job at the head of the buffer leaves first and the blocked sender's job slides into the freed slot (with an unbuffered channel, the worker takes it straight from the sender's hands). close(ch) wakes every parked receiver; buffered jobs still drain after close.
